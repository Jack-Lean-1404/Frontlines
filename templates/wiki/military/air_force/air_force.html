Air Force

The Air Force provides the primary aerial forces of the nations of Alterra. Aircraft allow nations to project military power across large distances, conduct air combat, support ground operations, and rapidly deploy forces across the battlefield.

Air operations are highly dependent on range, positioning, operational anchors, and air defence. Effective use of aircraft requires careful planning and coordination with friendly airports, tankers, and other supporting assets.

Air Forces

Air forces are organised around several different aircraft roles. Each aircraft type is designed to perform specific missions and contributes differently to military operations.

Aircraft Operations

Aircraft operate from friendly airports or suitable mobile aerial anchor points. An aircraft's operational range determines how far it may operate from its anchor point.

Airports provide the primary fixed bases for aerial operations. Tankers can function as mobile anchor points, allowing aircraft to operate farther from fixed infrastructure.

The destruction of an airport or tanker can have serious consequences for aircraft relying on that asset. Aircraft that cannot reach another suitable anchor point may be lost.

Air Combat

Air combat occurs when opposing aircraft engage during aerial operations. Aircraft may encounter hostile forces while entering or operating within contested airspace.

Aircraft can either engage enemy forces or bypass contested sectors. Different aircraft characteristics and special capabilities can significantly affect the outcome of aerial engagements.

Air Combat Learn how aerial engagements, contested airspace, and aircraft damage are resolved.

Air Defence

Air Defence protects friendly territory and military forces from hostile aircraft. Anti-Air units establish Air Defence Zones around their positions and automatically engage enemy aircraft that enter their coverage.

Aircraft classification also affects their ability to survive air defence engagements. Stealth aircraft are significantly harder for Anti-Air systems to detect and engage.

Air Defence Learn about Anti-Air, Air Defence Zones, stealth aircraft, and SEAD operations.

Specialised Air Operations

Some aircraft perform specialised missions that provide capabilities beyond conventional air combat.

These include airborne command and control, aerial refuelling, strategic airlift, and airdrop operations.

Strategic Airlift Learn how Strategic Airlift Aircraft transport ground forces and conduct airdrop operations.

Strategic Airlift

Strategic Airlift Aircraft provide rapid deployment of ground forces across long distances. They allow eligible ground units to be transported between friendly airbases or deployed directly into operational areas.

Strategic airlift can significantly reduce the time required to move forces between distant theatres and can provide commanders with additional flexibility during military operations.

Strategic Airlift Explore airlift, airdrop operations, operational limits, and Anti-Air interaction.

Air Superiority

Control of the airspace surrounding military operations can provide a significant strategic advantage.

Fighters can challenge enemy aircraft, while specialised aircraft such as AWACS provide enhanced situational awareness and coordination. Tankers extend the operational reach of friendly aircraft, while stealth aircraft can operate with reduced exposure to enemy air defences.

Maintaining air superiority therefore depends on more than simply fielding large numbers of aircraft. Range, support assets, air defence, and aircraft capabilities must all be considered when planning aerial operations.

Stealth

Stealth aircraft are specialised aerial assets designed to operate with a reduced probability of detection and engagement by enemy air defence systems.

Stealth provides significant advantages in both combat effectiveness and survivability. These aircraft are especially valuable when operating in areas protected by enemy Anti-Air systems.
