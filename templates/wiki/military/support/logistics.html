Logistics

Logistics represents the systems required to supply and sustain military forces during operations. Units require access to supplies in order to remain operational and participate effectively in combat.

Supply

Combat-capable units of a nation's armed forces require supplies to operate effectively. Modern military formations consume food, fuel, ammunition, and repair equipment while conducting operations.

Ground units can receive supplies through Logistics units or Cities. Naval units use a separate supply system and receive supplies from Supply Ships.

Logistics Units

Logistics units provide mobile supply support to friendly combat units operating away from established cities and supply networks.

A Logistics unit can supply any friendly combat unit that is the same size or smaller within a 2-sector radius.

For example, a Size 3 Logistics unit can supply friendly combat units of Size 1, Size 2, or Size 3 within its supply range. A Size 2 Logistics unit cannot supply a Size 3 combat unit.

Logistics Units Learn about Logistics formations and their role in maintaining ground forces in the field.

City Supply

Cities provide another source of supply for ground forces. A city can supply any unit stationed within its sector, regardless of the unit's size.

For a city to provide supply, a road must connect the city to the capital. The connecting road must also remain under friendly control.

This makes control of roads and territory an important part of maintaining military supply networks. Capturing or interrupting a key road can isolate forces from their source of supply.

Logistics Hub

The Logistics Hub is a specialised infrastructure facility designed to support military operations by providing a centralised distribution point for supplies, fuel, and equipment.

A Logistics Hub functions as a Level 5 Logistics Unit, automatically providing logistical support to friendly military forces within its operational range. This allows large formations to remain supplied without requiring dedicated mobile Logistics units to provide support.

Special Capability

The Logistics Hub automatically provides the same logistical support as a Level 5 Logistics Unit.

Supply Shortage

If a combat-capable unit does not receive the required supply, it enters a Supply Shortage.

Supply Shortage significantly restricts a unit's ability to conduct military operations.

  • Cannot attack.
  • Cannot defend.
  • Can move only 1 sector per turn.

Supply Shortage therefore makes maintaining supply lines essential during offensive operations, particularly when forces advance away from established cities and logistical infrastructure.

Naval Supply

Naval forces use a separate supply system from ground forces. All combat-capable naval units require supply to remain operational.

Unlike land forces, naval units can only receive supplies from Supply Ships.

Supply Ships

A Supply Ship can provide supplies to friendly naval units within a 2-sector radius, provided the Supply Ship is the same size or larger than the naval unit receiving supply.

For example, a Size 3 Supply Ship can supply Size 1, Size 2, and Size 3 naval units within its supply range. A Size 2 Supply Ship cannot supply a Size 3 naval unit.

Naval Supply Shortage

Naval units that do not receive supply enter a Supply Shortage and suffer the same basic operational restrictions as unsupplied ground forces.

  • Cannot attack.
  • Cannot defend.
  • Can move only 1 sector per turn.

Maintaining naval supply is therefore essential for fleets operating away from their normal bases and support networks.

Logistical Warfare

Supply is an important part of military operations in Frontlines. A force may possess significant combat strength while still becoming ineffective if its supply network is disrupted.

Players must therefore consider the position of Logistics units, the security of roads connecting cities to their capitals, and the location of Supply Ships when conducting operations far from home territory.

Protecting supply routes can be just as important as protecting combat formations. Disrupting an opponent's logistical network can restrict their movement and prevent their forces from attacking or defending.
