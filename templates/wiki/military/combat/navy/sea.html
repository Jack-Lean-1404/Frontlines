Naval Combat

Naval Combat governs engagements between opposing naval forces. Naval warfare allows nations to contest sea sectors, protect maritime routes, project military power, and support operations across coastal and overseas regions.

Naval combat uses the same general combat resolution system as other conventional combat. Naval formations rely on their Attack Strength and Defence Value to determine the outcome of engagements.

Initiating Combat

A naval unit may initiate combat against an enemy naval unit in an adjacent sector.

Naval units may not enter sectors occupied by enemy naval forces. Instead, the attacking force must stop in an adjacent sector and initiate combat against the opposing force.

A naval unit may move before attacking, but all movement must be completed before combat begins. Once combat has been initiated, the unit cannot resume movement during that turn.

Attack Strength

The attacking naval force uses its final Attack Strength when resolving combat.

Attack Strength represents the offensive capability of the attacking formation after all applicable modifiers have been applied.

Defence Value

The defending naval force uses its final Defence Value when resolving combat.

Defence Value represents the defensive capability of the defending formation after all applicable modifiers have been applied.

Combat Resolution

Naval combat uses an opposed roll system.

The attacking force makes a random roll between zero and its final Attack Strength. The defending force makes a random roll between zero and its final Defence Value.

The two results are compared to determine the outcome of the engagement.

01

Attacker

Rolls a random value between 0 and the final Attack Strength.

02

Defender

Rolls a random value between 0 and the final Defence Value.

Combat Outcome

The side with the higher combat roll wins the engagement. The losing naval formation suffers a reduction in formation size.

If the attacker rolls higher than the defender, the defending formation loses one size.

If the defender rolls higher than the attacker, the attacking formation loses one size.

If both sides roll the same result, both formations lose one size.

Naval Formation Size

Naval formation size represents the number of ships operating together as a single naval force.

When a naval formation loses one size, the number of ships in that formation is reduced by one level.

If a formation at its smallest available size suffers another size reduction, the formation is destroyed.

Naval Supply

Combat-capable naval units require supply to remain operational. Naval forces depend on Supply Ships to maintain their ability to conduct sustained operations at sea.

Units that fall into supply shortage suffer significant operational penalties, including restrictions on combat and movement.

Submarine Warfare

Submarine warfare encompasses both submarine operations and Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW). Submarines provide a powerful stealth-based threat against enemy naval forces, while specialised units are required to detect, engage, and destroy them.

Submarines can conduct high-risk attacks against enemy naval units, while Destroyers, other Submarines, and Marine Patrol Aircraft (MPA) provide the primary means of countering them.

Submarine Warfare Learn about submarine attacks, defensive evasion, and Anti-Submarine Warfare.

Air-to-Sea Combat allows specialised aircraft to conduct attacks against enemy naval forces.

Sealift Operations

Sealift allows naval forces to transport ground units across bodies of water. Sealift Ships allow nations to reinforce distant territories and support overseas military operations.

Transport capacity, loading, unloading, and the consequences of losing a transport are governed by specialised sealift rules.

Sealift Operations Learn about Sealift Ships, transport capacity, loading, unloading, and transport losses.

Amphibious Operations

Amphibious operations allow ground forces to be deployed from the sea onto coastal sectors. They provide a means of opening new fronts and establishing beachheads in enemy territory.

Amphibious assaults use specialised rules to determine whether a landing is successful and how contested landings affect transported ground forces.

Amphibious Operations Learn about Amphibious Assault Ships, beachheads, transport capacity, and contested landings.

Carrier Operations

Aircraft Carriers provide mobile air bases that allow Naval Multi-Role Fighters to operate far from land-based airports.

Carriers can also provide Anti-Air defence and support naval operations by extending the operational reach of friendly aircraft.

Carriers cannot directly attack enemy naval or ground units. Their offensive capability is provided by their embarked Naval Multi-Role Fighters.

Carrier Operations Learn about carrier aircraft capacity, Naval Multi-Role Fighters, and carrier-based operations.

Naval Warfare

Naval warfare is not limited to direct engagements between surface fleets. Successful naval operations require the effective use of combat forces, logistics, specialised units, and naval aviation.

Surface forces provide the primary means of direct naval combat, while Submarines, Carriers, Transport Ships, Supply Ships, and Amphibious Assault Ships provide specialised capabilities that extend the strategic flexibility of a fleet.
