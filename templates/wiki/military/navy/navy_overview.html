Navy

The Navy provides the primary maritime forces of the nations of Alterra. Naval units are responsible for controlling maritime territory, protecting sea lanes, supporting amphibious operations, and conducting offensive and defensive operations at sea.

Naval operations allow commanders to project military power across maritime sectors and influence coastal and oceanic areas. Different naval units provide different combinations of firepower, protection, mobility, and specialised capabilities.

Navy Units

The Navy consists of a range of vessels designed to perform different roles across the maritime battlefield. Each unit provides specialised capabilities that can be used independently or as part of a wider naval formation.

Effective naval operations depend on selecting appropriate vessels for the mission and coordinating them with friendly Army, Air Force, and Special Units.

Naval Warfare

Naval operations are centred around controlling maritime territory, engaging opposing naval forces, protecting friendly vessels, and supporting operations ashore.

Naval formations manoeuvre between maritime sectors while accounting for enemy positions, movement, range, and the specialised capabilities of individual vessels.

When opposing naval forces engage, the engagement is resolved using the naval combat system.

Sea Combat Learn how engagements between opposing naval forces are resolved.

Movement

Naval units move between maritime sectors a turn. A unit's Movement value determines how far it can normally travel, while other restrictions can affect where and how it moves.

Naval movement allows commanders to reposition fleets, reinforce threatened areas, intercept enemy forces, and establish control over important maritime sectors.

Movement must be completed before combat. Once a unit has initiated combat, it cannot resume movement during the same turn.

Movement Learn how military units move between sectors and how movement interacts with combat.

Submarine Warfare

Submarines provide a specialised naval capability by operating beneath the ocean surface.

Their ability to operate underwater allows them to conduct specialised attacks and operate differently from conventional surface vessels.

Sealift

Sealift allows military forces and equipment to be transported across maritime areas.

Transport vessels provide commanders with the ability to move forces over water and support operations across distant coastal areas.

Amphibious Operations

Amphibious operations allow naval forces to support the movement of military forces from sea onto coastal territory.

These operations allow commanders to establish a presence on enemy or contested coastlines and provide additional options for deploying ground forces.

Amphibious Operations Learn how naval forces support amphibious operations and coastal assaults.

Carrier Warfare

Aircraft carriers allow naval forces to project air power beyond the immediate reach of land-based aircraft.

Carrier operations combine naval and air power, allowing aircraft to operate from maritime positions and support wider fleet operations.

Carrier Warfare Learn how aircraft carriers support naval operations and project air power at sea.

Naval Logistics

Naval formations require logistical support to maintain sustained operations away from established bases.

Supply Ships provide logistical support to naval forces, allowing fleets to remain operational for longer periods and operate further from friendly infrastructure.

Maintaining naval logistics is particularly important during prolonged maritime operations.

Logistics Learn how military forces are supplied and sustained during operations.

Fleet Operations

Naval units are most effective when operated as part of coordinated formations.

Surface combatants can provide offensive and defensive firepower, submarines can provide specialised underwater capabilities, and support vessels can sustain fleets during extended operations.

Aircraft carriers can further extend the capabilities of a fleet by providing a mobile platform for naval aviation.

Combined Operations

Naval operations are most effective when coordinated with other branches of the military.

Naval forces can support Army operations along coastal areas, while Air Force aircraft can provide additional surveillance, combat power, and maritime capabilities.

Special Units can also complement naval forces through specialised capabilities such as submarines and Coastal Defence.

Naval Operations

Successful naval operations require commanders to consider vessel capabilities, movement, range, logistics, and the wider maritime environment.

Surface combatants can be used to engage enemy fleets and establish maritime control, while submarines provide specialised underwater capabilities.

Transport and Supply Ships allow naval forces to support military operations over greater distances and maintain sustained operations at sea.

Aircraft carriers provide an additional means of projecting air power from maritime positions and can support fleet operations across distant areas.

The effectiveness of a naval operation therefore depends not only on individual vessels, but also on how naval forces are organised and coordinated across the battlefield.
