Strategic Bombing

Strategic Bombing allows bomber aircraft to conduct attacks against enemy buildings. Successful bombing attacks destroy the targeted building.

Strategic Bombing provides the Air Force with the ability to directly attack enemy infrastructure and strategic buildings from the air. Bombing attacks are resolved separately from conventional air-to-air combat.

Eligible Aircraft

Only aircraft with the appropriate bombing capability can conduct Strategic Bombing attacks.

The following aircraft can conduct bombing attacks:

Bomber Conventional bomber aircraft capable of conducting strategic bombing attacks. Stealth Bomber Stealth bomber aircraft capable of conducting strategic bombing attacks.

Targeting

Strategic Bombing attacks are conducted against enemy buildings.

The bomber must be within its operational range of the targeted building before an attack can be conducted.

Buildings do not use formation size or a separate damage or hit-point system for Strategic Bombing.

Air Defence

Air Defence is resolved before the bombing attack.

The bomber must survive the Air Defence engagement before it can attempt to bomb the targeted building.

If the bomber is destroyed by Air Defence, the bombing attack does not occur.

Air Defence Learn about Anti-Air systems and Air Defence Zones.

Bombing Resolution

If the bomber survives Air Defence, the bombing attack is resolved using a 60% success chance.

A successful bombing attack destroys the targeted building.

A failed bombing attack has no effect and the targeted building remains intact.

Strategic Bombing Sequence

A Strategic Bombing attack follows a fixed sequence of events.

Bombing Attack Resolution
  1. The bomber selects an eligible enemy building within operational range.
  2. Air Defence is resolved against the bomber.
  3. If the bomber is destroyed, the attack ends.
  4. If the bomber survives, the bombing attack is resolved.
  5. The bomber has a 60% chance of successfully bombing the target.
  6. If successful, the targeted building is destroyed.
  7. If unsuccessful, the targeted building is unaffected.

Building Destruction

Strategic Bombing uses a binary building destruction system rather than a conventional damage system.

Buildings do not have a separate bombing HP or damage value, and formation size does not affect the bombing result.

A successful bombing attack destroys the targeted building completely. A failed attack causes no damage and leaves the building intact.

Example

A BOMB aircraft targets an eligible enemy building that is within its operational range.

Air Defence is resolved first. The bomber survives and proceeds with the bombing attack.

The bombing attack is then given its 60% chance of success. If successful, the targeted building is destroyed. If the attack fails, the building remains intact.

Strategic Bombing Rules

  • Aircraft: BOMB and SBOMB can conduct Strategic Bombing.
  • Target: Eligible enemy buildings.
  • Range: The bomber must be within operational range of the target.
  • Air Defence: Resolved before the bombing attack.
  • Success Chance: 60% if the bomber survives Air Defence.
  • Success: The targeted building is destroyed.
  • Failure: No effect.
  • Building HP: Buildings do not use a separate damage or hit-point system.
  • Formation Size: Formation size does not affect building destruction.
