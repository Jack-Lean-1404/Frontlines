Military

The military system in Frontlines represents the armed forces of Alterra's nations and alliances across land, sea, and air. Players command military forces, manoeuvre units across the map, conduct combat operations, and maintain the logistics and command networks required to sustain their forces.

Military Forces

Military forces are divided into four primary categories: Ground Units, Naval Units, Aerial Units, and Special Units. Each category contains different units designed to perform specific roles during military operations.

Successful military operations require players to combine different types of forces. Combat units provide the primary offensive and defensive capabilities, while support units provide logistics, command, transport, and other force-multiplication effects.

Combat

Combat occurs when opposing forces engage during the Action Phase. Different forms of combat use different rules depending on the units involved.

Standard combat uses an opposed roll system. Attacking units use their final Attack Strength while defending units use their final Defence Value. Relevant modifiers are applied before the combat is resolved.

Combat Mechanics Learn how combat is initiated, resolved, and modified across land, sea, and air operations.

Movement and Operations

Military units move between adjacent sectors at any time during a turn. A unit's Movement value determines how many sectors it may normally traverse, while terrain and special rules can modify movement or restrict access to certain sectors.

Military operations also include specialised activities such as strategic airlift, sealift, amphibious operations, and carrier operations.

Logistics and Command

Military forces require supplies and effective command to maintain their combat effectiveness. Logistics units and cities provide supply to eligible forces, while Headquarters provide command and coordination across the battlefield.

Units without supply suffer severe operational restrictions. Units operating outside the command range of a suitable Headquarters become Disorganised and suffer reduced effectiveness.

Logistics Learn how supply is provided and what happens when units become unsupplied. Command & Control Learn how Headquarters command friendly forces and how Disorganisation affects units.

Military Strategy

Success in Frontlines depends on more than the strength of individual units. Players must coordinate combined arms, maintain supply networks, protect important infrastructure, and choose where and when to commit their forces.

Control of territory, strategic objectives, ports, airports, and other important locations can determine the success of military operations. Maintaining the ability to reinforce and sustain forces is equally important during prolonged campaigns.
