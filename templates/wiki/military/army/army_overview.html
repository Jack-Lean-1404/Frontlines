Army

The Army provides the primary ground forces of the nations of Alterra. Army units are responsible for capturing, defending, and controlling territory while conducting offensive and defensive operations across the battlefield.

Ground operations require the coordination of combat formations, support units, logistics, command, and movement. Different Army units are designed to perform specific roles within a combined-arms force.

Army Forces

Army forces are divided into two primary categories: Combat Units and Support Units. Combat units provide the primary fighting capability of ground forces, while support units provide specialised capabilities that allow formations to operate effectively.

Combat Units

Combat units form the core of Army formations. They provide the primary offensive and defensive capabilities required to engage enemy forces and control territory.

Different combat formations have different capabilities, allowing commanders to build forces suited to the terrain and operational situation.

Support Units

Support units provide specialised capabilities that allow Army formations to operate effectively and sustain military operations.

Engineers, logistics units, and Headquarters provide capabilities that complement frontline combat formations.

Land Warfare

Army operations are centred around controlling territory and defeating opposing ground forces.

Ground formations must manoeuvre across sectors while accounting for terrain, movement costs, enemy positions, supply, and command.

When opposing ground forces engage, the engagement is resolved using the land combat system.

Land Combat Learn how engagements between opposing ground forces are resolved.

Movement

Army units move between sectors during the Action Phase. A unit's Movement value determines how far it can normally travel, while terrain and other restrictions can affect where and how it moves.

Movement must be completed before combat. Once a unit has initiated combat, it cannot resume movement during the same phase.

Movement Learn how military units move between sectors and how movement interacts with combat. Terrain Learn how terrain affects movement costs and unit access.

Logistics

Army formations require logistical support to maintain sustained operations in the field.

Logistics units and infrastructure provide supply to eligible forces, allowing military formations to operate away from established infrastructure.

Logistics Learn how military forces are supplied and sustained during operations.

Command & Control

Army formations operate more effectively when supported by an appropriate command structure.

Headquarters provide command and coordination to friendly forces within their effective range. Units operating outside suitable command support can become Disorganised and suffer reduced effectiveness.

Command & Control Learn how Headquarters coordinate friendly forces and how Disorganisation affects units.

Combined Arms

Army operations are most effective when different unit types are used together.

Infantry, mechanised formations, armoured forces, and support units each provide different capabilities. Coordinating these forces allows commanders to exploit the strengths of individual units while reducing their weaknesses.
