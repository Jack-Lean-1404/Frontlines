Air Force

The Air Force provides fixed-wing aerial capabilities for nations across Alterra. Aircraft can conduct air combat, strike enemy forces, provide reconnaissance, and support military operations across the battlefield.

Air Power

Air forces provide military commanders with the ability to project combat power across large distances and respond rapidly to developments on the battlefield.

Aircraft can operate independently of ground formations and can reach targets that may otherwise be difficult to engage using conventional ground forces.

Effective air operations require careful management of aircraft range, bases, airspace, and supporting forces.

Air Superiority

Air superiority represents the ability of a nation's air forces to operate effectively while limiting the enemy's ability to do the same.

Fighter aircraft play the primary role in establishing and maintaining air superiority by engaging hostile aircraft.

Control of the air can significantly improve the effectiveness of other military operations by allowing friendly aircraft to operate with reduced opposition.

Air Combat

Aircraft can engage opposing aircraft during aerial combat. Air combat is governed by aircraft capabilities, applicable combat rules, and the conditions under which the engagement takes place.

Fighter aircraft are primarily intended for air-to-air combat, while other aircraft may have limited or specialised capabilities for defending themselves against hostile aircraft.

Air Combat Learn how aerial engagements are initiated and resolved.

Ground Attack

Aircraft can also support ground operations by attacking enemy land forces and other military targets.

Ground-attack missions allow air forces to contribute to land operations without requiring aircraft to operate from the same sectors as friendly ground formations.

Different aircraft types have different capabilities and effectiveness when conducting attacks against ground targets.

Strategic Bombing

Bombers provide long-range strike capability against strategic targets.

Strategic bombing can be used to attack important enemy infrastructure and military assets, weakening an opponent's ability to sustain its war effort.

Aircraft Range

Aircraft have a defined operational range that determines which sectors they can reach from their operating base.

Aircraft range must be considered when planning missions. A target outside an aircraft's operational range cannot be reached by that aircraft.

Air Bases

Aircraft require suitable infrastructure from which to operate.

Air bases provide the operational infrastructure required to deploy and maintain aircraft during military operations.

The location of air bases is strategically important because it determines the areas in which aircraft can effectively operate.

Air Defence

Air forces must also contend with enemy air-defence systems. Anti-Air units can protect sectors from hostile aircraft and create areas in which aircraft operations become more dangerous.

Aircraft may require specialised capabilities to operate effectively against defended targets.

Air Defence Learn how Anti-Air units and Air Defence Zones protect against hostile aircraft.

Aircraft Operations

Air forces can conduct a variety of missions depending on the aircraft available and the strategic situation.

Air Combat Conduct aerial engagements against enemy aircraft. Ground Attack Use aircraft to attack enemy ground formations and military targets. Strategic Bombing Conduct long-range attacks against strategic targets.

Air Force and Logistics

Air operations depend on appropriate logistical support and infrastructure. Aircraft operating from distant bases must be supported by the resources required to maintain sustained operations.

Air Force and Command

Air operations are also affected by the wider command and control system. Maintaining effective coordination between aircraft and supporting forces is an important part of military operations.
