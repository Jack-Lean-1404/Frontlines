Artillery

Artillery is a key component of modern warfare, delivering powerful and sustained fire over long distances. It enhances combined arms operations and provides crucial support to frontline units.

Artillery Combat

Artillery provides indirect fire against enemy ground forces, allowing artillery units to engage targets without directly entering the sector occupied by the enemy.

Artillery attacks are resolved using a specialised artillery combat system. Unlike standard land combat, artillery attacks determine whether the defending unit is suppressed, destroyed, or unaffected.

Movement and Range

Regular artillery must spend 1 Movement Point to conduct an artillery attack.

Mechanised Artillery may conduct an artillery attack without spending Movement Points.

Artillery units can attack any enemy ground unit within 2 sectors of the firing artillery unit.

Artillery Attack Resolution

When an artillery attack is successful, the defending unit's outcome is determined using a 50/25/25 roll.

The three possible outcomes are Suppressed, Destroyed, and No Effect.

50%

Suppressed

The defending unit cannot move during its next turn.

25%

Destroyed

The defending unit is removed from play.

25%

No Effect

The artillery attack misses and nothing happens to the defending unit.

Suppression

A suppressed unit cannot move during its next turn.

Suppression temporarily prevents the affected unit from repositioning, allowing artillery to disrupt enemy manoeuvres and restrict the movement of opposing forces.

Destruction

If the artillery attack results in destruction, the defending unit is removed from play.

Artillery can therefore eliminate an enemy unit directly without requiring a conventional ground assault against its sector.

No Effect

A No Effect result means that the artillery attack missed. Nothing happens to the defending unit.

If an artillery attack is unsuccessful, the defender remains unaffected.

The attacking artillery unit is never affected by its own artillery attack.

Counter-Battery Fire

Counter-battery fire allows artillery units to automatically respond whenever an enemy artillery unit fires.

Whenever a friendly artillery unit fires, any enemy artillery units within 2 sectors of the firing artillery may automatically perform a counter-battery attack against it.

Counter-battery fire occurs regardless of the outcome of the original artillery attack. It is not dependent on whether the original attack hits, misses, suppresses, or destroys its target.

An artillery unit may also perform counter-battery fire regardless of whether it has already conducted its own artillery attack during the turn. Counter-battery fire is a separate automatic response and does not require the responding artillery unit to have unused movement or an unused attack.

In other words, whenever eligible enemy artillery is within range of a firing artillery unit, the counter-battery attack occurs regardless of what that artillery unit has already done during the turn.

Counter-Battery Rules

Counter-battery attacks do not require Movement Points.

Counter-battery fire uses the normal artillery combat rules. The responding artillery unit therefore has the same possible outcomes:

  • Suppressed
  • Destroyed
  • No Effect

Multiple enemy artillery units may perform counter-battery attacks simultaneously if they are within 2 sectors of the firing artillery unit.

Counter-Battery Example

A Size 3 artillery unit fires at an enemy Infantry unit. An enemy Size 2 artillery unit is located within 2 sectors of the firing artillery.

The enemy Size 2 artillery unit automatically performs a counter-battery attack against the Size 3 artillery unit.

This counter-battery attack occurs regardless of whether the original artillery attack was successful or unsuccessful.

The enemy Size 2 artillery unit can perform this counter-battery attack even if it has already conducted its own artillery attack during the same turn. The counter-battery attack is automatic and is not prevented by the unit having already attacked.

If multiple enemy artillery units are within 2 sectors of the firing artillery unit, each eligible artillery unit may perform its own counter-battery attack.

Artillery and Combined Arms

Artillery enhances combined arms operations by providing long-range fire support to frontline forces.

Artillery can suppress enemy formations, destroy units, and disrupt opposing operations before or during a wider military engagement.

The ability to attack from a distance allows artillery to support frontline formations without directly entering the enemy's sector.

Artillery Positioning

Artillery positioning is an important consideration during military operations. Firing positions must be close enough to enemy forces to remain within the 2-sector artillery range.

At the same time, artillery units must account for enemy artillery positions and the possibility of automatic counter-battery fire.

Mechanised Artillery provides additional flexibility because it can attack without spending Movement Points, allowing it to retain its movement capability while conducting fire.

Artillery and Movement

Artillery follows the standard movement rules unless a unit-specific rule states otherwise.

Regular artillery must spend 1 Movement Point when conducting an attack, while Mechanised Artillery can attack without spending Movement Points.

Other movement restrictions, including Supply Shortage and Disorganisation, may still affect artillery units.

Artillery and Logistics

Artillery units require logistical support to remain effective during military operations.

Supply is particularly important for artillery operating away from established cities and logistical infrastructure.

Artillery and Command

Artillery units are also subject to the command system. Operating outside the command range of a suitable Headquarters can cause a unit to become Disorganised.

Disorganisation reduces a unit's combat effectiveness and restricts its movement.
