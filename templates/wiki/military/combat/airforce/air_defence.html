Air Defence

Air Defence protects military forces and strategic areas from hostile aircraft. Anti-Air units and aircraft carriers establish Air Defence Zones that automatically engage enemy aircraft that interact with the protected area.

Air Defence is designed to deny enemy air operations and force aircraft to consider the risks of operating within defended areas.

Air Defence Units

Air Defence is provided by dedicated Anti-Air units and Aircraft Carriers.

Anti-Air Learn about Anti-Air units and their role in protecting against hostile aircraft. Carrier Learn about Carriers and their naval Air Defence capabilities.

Air Defence Zone

Each Anti-Air unit and Carrier establishes an Air Defence Zone extending 1 sector around its position.

The Air Defence Zone remains the same regardless of the formation size of the Air Defence unit.

An aircraft does not need to end its movement inside the zone to be engaged. Any interaction with an Air Defence Zone can trigger an engagement.

Air Defence Engagement

When an Anti-Air unit engages an enemy air unit within its Air Defence Zone, the aircraft must resolve an evasion check based on its classification.

Non-stealth air units have an 80% chance of being hit by Anti-Air fire.

Stealth air units have a 20% chance of being hit by Anti-Air fire, reflecting their reduced detectability and advanced countermeasures.

Aircraft Damage

When an Air Defence attack successfully hits an aircraft, the aircraft suffers one formation size loss.

If an aircraft is already at its smallest available formation size and suffers another size loss, the aircraft is destroyed.

Air Defence damage therefore directly reduces the effective strength of an aircraft formation.

Air Defence Formation Size

The formation size of an Anti-Air unit does not change its Air Defence Zone or its chance to hit aircraft.

The only effect of losing formation size is a reduction in the unit's effective Hit Points.

Layered Air Defence

Multiple Air Defence units can protect the same area, allowing players to establish layered Air Defence networks.

If an aircraft enters an area covered by multiple enemy Air Defence Zones, each applicable Air Defence unit engages the aircraft independently.

Layered Air Defence can therefore create significantly greater protection around important military positions and strategic areas.

Helicopters

Attack Helicopters cannot be directly targeted by ground-based Anti-Air units.

Their low-altitude nap-of-the-earth flight allows them to operate below the engagement capability of ground-based Air Defence systems.

Rotary Warfare Learn about Attack Helicopters and their specialised battlefield operations.

Suppression of Enemy Air Defences (SEAD)

SEAD (Suppression of Enemy Air Defences) is a specialised air operation used to attack and destroy enemy Anti-Air units.

When an aircraft conducts a SEAD mission against an enemy Anti-Air unit, the Anti-Air unit fires first and attempts to destroy the attacking aircraft.

If the attacking aircraft survives, it has a 60% chance to destroy the targeted Anti-Air unit.

The SEAD destruction chance is fixed at 60% and is not affected by the Strength or Defence of either the attacking aircraft or the Anti-Air unit.

Stealth aircraft are particularly effective at conducting SEAD missions because they are significantly harder for Anti-Air systems to hit.

Strategic Role

Air Defence is essential for protecting important military positions, infrastructure, and forces from enemy air power.

Effective Air Defence networks can restrict enemy aircraft movement and make certain areas significantly more difficult to operate within.

However, Air Defence must be positioned carefully and protected from ground attacks and SEAD operations.
