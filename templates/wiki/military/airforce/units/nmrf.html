Naval Multirole Fighter

AIR

Overview

Naval Multirole Fighters are versatile combat aircraft designed to operate in support of naval forces. They combine air-to-air combat capability with the ability to conduct attacks against enemy ground and naval targets.

Role

The primary role of the Naval Multirole Fighter is to provide flexible air support for naval operations. NMRFs can engage enemy aircraft, conduct Air-to-Ground attacks, and perform Anti-Ship operations against enemy naval forces.

Strengths

  • Highly versatile combat aircraft.
  • Capable of engaging enemy aircraft.
  • Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks.
  • Capable of conducting Anti-Ship attacks.
  • Effective in support of naval operations.

Weaknesses

  • Less specialised than dedicated air superiority aircraft.
  • Moderate combat capability.
  • Requires significant resources and support to operate.
  • Less effective in specialised roles than aircraft dedicated to those missions.

Air-to-Sea Combat

Naval Multirole Fighters can conduct Anti-Ship attacks against eligible enemy naval units. This allows NMRFs to directly engage enemy surface forces without requiring a friendly naval unit to engage them first.

Air-to-Sea Combat Learn how NMRFs conduct Anti-Ship attacks.

Air-to-Ground Combat

Naval Multirole Fighters can also conduct attacks against eligible enemy ground forces, allowing them to provide flexible air support during land operations.

Air-to-Ground Combat Learn how aircraft conduct attacks against enemy ground forces.

Recommended Employment

Use Naval Multirole Fighters to provide flexible air support across multiple operational roles. They are particularly valuable for supporting naval forces, defending against enemy aircraft, and conducting Anti-Ship attacks against enemy surface formations.
