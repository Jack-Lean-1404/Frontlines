Movement

Movement determines how military units manoeuvre across the battlefield. Units move between sectors using Movement Points to travel across connected territory. Terrain, rivers, and certain operational conditions can affect how far a unit can move.

Sector Movement

The Frontlines map is divided into sectors. Military units move from one sector to an adjacent sector during a turn.

A unit's available Movement Points determine how many sectors it can traverse during a turn. Moving into a sector consumes Movement Points according to the movement cost of that sector.

Movement Points

Each unit has a Movement value that determines the number of Movement Points available to it during its turn.

Movement Points are spent when a unit moves between sectors. Different terrain types have different movement costs, meaning that the same unit may be able to travel different distances depending on the terrain it crosses.

Terrain Movement Costs

Terrain affects the cost of moving between sectors. Difficult terrain requires more Movement Points and can therefore slow the advance of military formations.

Players must account for terrain when planning advances, retreats, and the movement of reinforcements toward the frontline.

Terrain Learn how different terrain types affect military movement and combat.

River Crossings

Rivers impose additional movement requirements on military forces attempting to cross them.

Engineers can assist friendly forces with river crossings, reducing the difficulty associated with crossing rivers.

River crossings can therefore become important tactical considerations when planning an advance or establishing a defensive position.

Engineers Learn about the engineering capabilities provided by Engineer units.

Sector Capacity

Sectors have a limit on the number of military units that can occupy them. A maximum of two units per domain may occupy a sector.

This limit applies separately to each military domain, allowing ground, naval, and aerial forces to operate within their respective sectors without sharing a single universal capacity.

Enemy Sectors

Military units cannot simply move into sectors occupied by enemy forces. Entering enemy-held territory requires the appropriate combat or operational action rather than normal movement.

This distinction separates normal manoeuvre from offensive operations and prevents units from bypassing hostile forces through ordinary movement.

Zones of Control

Frontlines does not use traditional Zones of Control.

Units therefore do not automatically restrict the movement of neighbouring enemy units simply by occupying a sector. Movement is instead governed by sector connections, Movement Points, terrain, enemy occupation, and the other applicable movement rules.

Movement Restrictions

A unit's effective movement can be reduced by other military mechanics. For example, units suffering Supply Shortage or Disorganisation are restricted to moving only one sector per turn.

Logistics Learn how Supply Shortage affects military movement and operations. Command & Control Learn how Disorganisation affects the movement and effectiveness of military units.

Movement and Operations

Movement is a fundamental part of military operations. Successful manoeuvre requires players to consider available Movement Points, terrain, river crossings, sector capacity, supply, and command coverage.

Careful movement planning allows forces to concentrate at important positions, reinforce threatened sectors, and maintain the logistical and command networks required to support advancing formations.
