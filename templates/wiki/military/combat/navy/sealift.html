Sealift Operations

Sealift Operations allow nations to transport ground forces across bodies of water using Sealift Ships. Sealift provides the ability to reinforce distant territories, reposition military formations, and support overseas operations.

Sealift Ships are specialised naval units designed to move ground forces by sea. Their ability to carry military formations makes them essential for projecting ground forces beyond their home territory.

Transport Capacity

A Sealift Ship may carry one ground unit that is the same size or smaller than the Sealift Ship.

Only one ground unit may be carried by a Sealift Ship at any given time.

Sealift capacity is determined by the size of the Sealift Ship.

01

Size 1

Can carry one Size 1 ground unit.

02

Size 2

Can carry one Size 2, Size 1, or smaller ground unit.

03

Size 3

Can carry one Size 3, Size 2, or Size 1 ground unit.

Loading

A ground unit may be loaded onto a Sealift Ship when the Sealift Ship is able to carry that unit.

Loading may only be performed in a sector containing a City, as the required city infrastructure is necessary to support the transfer of military forces between land and sea.

Loading a ground unit normally consumes the Sealift Ship's action.

A Port removes this movement or action cost, allowing units to embark onto Sealift Ships without the normal movement penalty.

Unloading

A Sealift Ship may unload its transported ground unit when conducting a sealift operation.

Unloading may only be performed in a sector containing a City.

Unloading a ground unit normally consumes the Sealift Ship's action.

A Port removes this movement or action cost, allowing transported units to disembark without the normal movement penalty.

Sealift Ship Loss

Sealift Ships are vulnerable while carrying ground forces. The loss of a Sealift Ship can therefore result in the loss of the formation it is transporting.

If a Sealift Ship is destroyed while carrying a ground unit, the transported ground unit is immediately destroyed as well.

Transport Damage

If a Sealift Ship is reduced in formation size while carrying a ground unit, the Sealift unit is reduced by the same number of formation-size levels.

For example, if a Sealift Ship is reduced from Size 3 to Size 2, the transported ground unit is also reduced by one size level.

If the transported ground unit is already at its smallest size and would be reduced further, the transported unit is destroyed.

Strategic Use

Sealift allows nations to move ground forces across large bodies of water without requiring a continuous land route.

Sealift Ships can therefore be used to reinforce isolated territories, establish overseas military presence, and support amphibious and wider naval operations.

Because transported forces are vulnerable if their Sealift Ship is destroyed, protecting sealift routes and Sealift vessels is an important part of overseas military operations.
