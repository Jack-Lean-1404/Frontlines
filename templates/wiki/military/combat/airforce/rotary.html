Rotary Warfare

Rotary Warfare governs the combat and battlefield operations of Attack Helicopters. Attack Helicopters provide direct close air support to ground forces and conduct specialised anti-armour operations against enemy formations.

Attack Helicopters

Attack Helicopters are specialised rotary-wing aircraft designed to operate directly in support of ground forces.

Their primary roles are providing close air support and conducting attacks against concentrated enemy ground forces, particularly armoured formations.

Attack Helicopter Learn about Attack Helicopter capabilities, statistics, and unit-specific rules.

Operational Range

Attack Helicopters may attack enemy ground units within 3 sectors of a friendly Airfield or Airport.

Their operational range is measured from the friendly airfield or airport serving as their operational anchor.

Low-Altitude Operations

Attack Helicopters can operate at low altitude using nap-of-the-earth (NOE) flight.

Because of this operating profile, Attack Helicopters cannot be directly targeted by ground-based Anti-Air-capable units.

Ground Attack

When attacking enemy ground units, Attack Helicopters use the same general combat resolution rules as land units.

The helicopter's attack is resolved against the defending ground unit using the applicable combat values.

Attack Risk

Attack Helicopters remain vulnerable while conducting attacks against ground forces.

Each time an Attack Helicopter conducts an attack, there is a 25% chance that the helicopter is destroyed, regardless of the outcome of its attack.

This represents the risks associated with operating close to enemy forces and ground-based threats.

Air-to-Air Combat

Attack Helicopters can still be targeted and attacked by enemy aircraft.

When engaged by enemy aircraft, Attack Helicopters follow the normal air-to-air combat rules.

Air Combat Learn about aircraft engagements and air-to-air combat.

Strategic Role

Attack Helicopters provide a mobile form of battlefield air support capable of operating close to friendly ground forces.

Their ability to attack ground formations while remaining protected from direct ground-based Anti-Air engagement makes them particularly useful for supporting frontline operations.

However, their vulnerability to enemy aircraft and the risk associated with every ground attack means they must be used carefully.
