Amphibious Assault Ship

SEA

Overview

Amphibious Assault Ships are large naval vessels designed to transport and deploy ground forces from the sea. They provide the primary naval capability for conducting amphibious operations and establishing beachheads on coastal sectors.

Role

The primary role of the Amphibious Assault Ship is to transport eligible ground forces and deploy them onto coastal sectors. They allow armies to open new fronts and conduct operations beyond their existing land borders.

Strengths

  • Enables amphibious operations.
  • Provides substantial capacity for transporting ground forces.
  • Allows new fronts to be established across coastal sectors.
  • Large formation size provides significant transport capacity.

Weaknesses

  • Very expensive to construct and maintain.
  • Limited combat capability compared with dedicated surface combatants.
  • High-value target during amphibious operations.
  • Requires protection from enemy naval and air forces.

Amphibious Operations

Amphibious Assault Ships provide the naval capability required to deploy ground forces from the sea onto coastal sectors.

Amphibious operations can be used to establish beachheads and open new fronts in enemy territory. Specialised rules determine whether a landing succeeds and how transported forces are affected during contested landings.

Amphibious Operations Learn about Amphibious Assault Ships, beachheads, transport capacity, and contested landings.

Transport Capacity

An Amphibious Assault Ship can carry ground units up to its own Size.

The number of ground units an AAS can transport is also determined by its size. An AAS can carry a number of ground units equal to its own Size, provided each transported unit is the same size or smaller.

Recommended Employment

Use Amphibious Assault Ships to transport ground forces across the sea and establish new fronts on coastal sectors. They should normally operate alongside naval combatants that can protect them from enemy surface, submarine, and air threats.

Due to their high cost and strategic importance, Amphibious Assault Ships should be protected carefully during both transit and landing operations.
