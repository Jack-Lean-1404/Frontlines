Land Combat

Land Combat governs engagements between opposing ground forces. Combat is resolved using the attacking force's Attack Strength and the defending force's Defence Value, with applicable modifiers determined before the engagement is resolved.

Initiating Combat

A ground unit may initiate combat against an enemy unit in an adjacent sector.

Units may not enter sectors occupied by enemy forces. Instead, the attacking unit must stop in an adjacent sector and initiate combat against the opposing force.

A unit may move before attacking, but all movement must be completed before combat begins. Once combat has been initiated, the unit cannot resume movement during that turn.

Attack Strength

The attacking force uses its final Attack Strength when resolving a land combat engagement.

Attack Strength represents the offensive capability of the attacking formation after all applicable modifiers have been applied.

Defence Value

The defending force uses its final Defence Value when resolving the engagement.

Defence Value represents the defensive capability of the defending formation after all applicable modifiers have been applied.

Combat Resolution

Land combat uses an opposed roll system.

The attacking force makes a random roll between zero and its final Attack Strength. The defending force makes a random roll between zero and its final Defence Value.

The two results are compared to determine the outcome of the engagement.

01

Attacker

Rolls a random value between 0 and the final Attack Strength.

02

Defender

Rolls a random value between 0 and the final Defence Value.

Combat Outcome

The side with the higher combat roll wins the engagement. The losing formation suffers a reduction in formation size.

If the attacker rolls higher than the defender, the defending formation loses one size.

If the defender rolls higher than the attacker, the attacking formation loses one size.

If both sides roll the same result, both formations lose one size.

Formation Size Loss

Combat damage is represented through reductions in formation size rather than a separate hit-point system.

When a formation loses one size, it moves to the next smaller formation level.

If a formation at its smallest available size suffers another size loss, the formation is destroyed.

Combat Destruction

A formation is destroyed when it suffers a size reduction while already at its smallest available formation size.

Destroyed formations are removed from the battlefield and can no longer participate in subsequent combat or movement.

Multiple Attacks

Multiple friendly units may participate in an attack against an enemy force occupying an adjacent sector.

Coordinating multiple formations allows a player to concentrate combat power against a single opposing formation.

The exact resolution of multiple-unit attacks depends on the applicable combat rules and participating unit types.

Terrain and Combat

Terrain can affect the effectiveness of ground forces during combat. Units may receive different advantages or penalties depending on the terrain in which an engagement occurs.

Terrain can therefore influence both the movement of forces toward an engagement and the outcome of the engagement itself.

Terrain Learn how different terrain types affect military movement and combat.

Armoured and Mechanised Forces

Armoured and Mechanised formations provide specialised capabilities during land combat.

When an Armoured unit is in the same sector as a Mechanised Infantry unit, the Armoured unit gains a +1 bonus to the Strength roll per unit level during combat.

When a Mechanised Infantry unit is in the same sector as an Armoured unit, the Mechanised Infantry unit gains a +1 bonus to the Damage roll per unit level during combat.

Breakthrough

If an Armoured or Mechanised unit attacks a sector and, as a result of that combat, all defending units in the sector are destroyed, that unit may immediately move into the now-empty sector. This is known as a Breakthrough.

A Breakthrough may only occur as a direct result of that unit’s own attack and only into the sector it just cleared. No further combat is conducted as part of this movement and does not cost Movement Points.

Artillery

Artillery provides long-range fire support for friendly ground forces. Artillery units can engage enemy ground formations without requiring direct contact with the target.

Their ability to attack from a distance makes artillery particularly valuable for supporting frontline formations, disrupting enemy positions, and influencing the battlefield beyond the immediate frontline.
