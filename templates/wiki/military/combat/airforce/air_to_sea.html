Air-to-Sea Combat

Air-to-Sea Combat governs attacks conducted by specialised aircraft against eligible enemy naval forces. Aircraft must first survive any applicable air defence or interception before resolving their attack against the naval target.

Eligible Targets

Aircraft may conduct Air-to-Sea attacks against eligible enemy naval units within their operational range.

Air-to-Sea attacks are directed against enemy naval forces and are resolved using the naval unit's current Defence.

Air-to-Sea Capable Units

Only specialised aircraft are capable of conducting Air-to-Sea attacks:

Naval Multi-Role Fighter Specialised naval aircraft capable of conducting Anti-Ship attacks against enemy naval units. Marine Patrol Aircraft Maritime patrol aircraft capable of conducting Anti-Ship attacks against enemy naval units.

Air Defence and Interception

Enemy air defence and interception are resolved before the Air-to-Sea attack takes place.

The aircraft must survive these defensive engagements in order to conduct its attack against the naval target.

Aircraft Damage

If an aircraft is hit by enemy air defence or interception, it immediately suffers one formation size loss.

Aircraft formation damage is caused by effects such as air defence and interception, rather than simply losing an Air-to-Sea combat roll.

Destroyed Aircraft

If an aircraft is destroyed during air defence or interception, its Air-to-Sea attack does not occur.

Only aircraft that survive the defensive engagement may continue to resolve their Air-to-Sea attack.

Air-to-Sea Combat Roll

A surviving aircraft resolves its attack against the target's Defence using the aircraft's Strength.

Both the attacking aircraft and defending naval unit make their respective combat rolls using their current combat values.

01

Aircraft

Rolls using its current Strength.

02

Naval Unit

Rolls using its current Defence.

Attack Outcome

If the aircraft's attack roll is higher than the target's Defence roll, the attack is successful and the target suffers one formation size loss.

If the target's Defence roll is higher than the aircraft's attack roll, the attack has no effect.

If both rolls are equal, the attack has no effect.

Formation Size Loss

A formation size loss moves the affected naval unit to its next smaller formation level.

Formation size reductions represent losses to the formation and reduce its combat effectiveness according to the applicable formation rules.

Destruction

If a formation at its smallest available formation level suffers another formation size loss, the formation is destroyed.

Destroyed formations are removed from play.

Aircraft Combat Damage

Aircraft do not suffer formation size loss simply because they lose an Air-to-Sea combat roll.

Aircraft formation damage is caused by effects such as air defence and interception.

Combat Effectiveness

Reducing a naval formation's size reduces its combat effectiveness according to the applicable formation rules.

Attack Sequence

Air-to-Sea attacks follow a defined sequence to determine whether the aircraft successfully strikes its naval target.

01

Defensive Engagement

Enemy air defence and interception are resolved.

02

Aircraft Survival

The aircraft suffers damage or is destroyed if affected by the defensive engagement.

03

Attack Roll

A surviving aircraft rolls against the naval target's Defence.

04

Resolution

A successful attack reduces the naval formation's formation size.

Anti-Ship Operations

Air-to-Sea attacks provide naval forces with an aerial threat that can be projected across large areas of the battlefield.

Naval Multi-Role Fighters and Marine Patrol Aircraft allow commanders to attack enemy naval formations without requiring a friendly surface fleet to directly engage the target.

Effective Anti-Ship operations require careful use of aircraft operational range and consideration of enemy Air Defence and interception.
