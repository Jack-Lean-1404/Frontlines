Strategic Airlift & Airdropping

Strategic Airlift Aircraft (SAA) provide rapid deployment of ground forces across long distances. They allow eligible ground formations to be transported between friendly airbases or deployed directly into operational areas through airdrops.

Strategic Airlift Aircraft

Strategic Airlift Aircraft are specialised transport aircraft designed to rapidly move ground forces over long distances.

An SAA can transport eligible ground units and Attack Helicopters up to its own formation size. Armoured units cannot be transported.

For example, a Size 3 SAA can transport a Size 3, Size 2, or Size 1 eligible unit, excluding Armoured units.

Operational Range

Strategic Airlift Aircraft have an operational range of 10 sectors from an Airport or Tanker.

Tankers may therefore be used as operational anchor points for SAA operations.

Air Combat Learn about aircraft operational range, airports, and tankers.

Standard Airlift

Standard Airlift allows ground units to be transported between friendly airbases using Strategic Airlift Aircraft.

Units transported using Standard Airlift are not subject to suppression, formation-size reduction, or destruction during the transport.

Airdropping

Strategic Airlift Aircraft can conduct airdrop operations to deploy eligible ground units directly into friendly, contested, or enemy-controlled sectors.

Airdropping allows ground forces to be deployed directly into an operational area without requiring a conventional airbase at the destination.

Airdrop Mishaps

Airdrop operations carry a risk of logistical and operational mishaps.

There is a 20% chance that a unit deployed by airdrop is reduced to its next smaller formation size.

The Strategic Airlift Aircraft itself is not destroyed by an airdrop mishap, even if the transported unit is damaged or eliminated.

Anti-Air Interaction

Strategic Airlift Aircraft remain vulnerable to enemy Air Defence while conducting airdrop operations.

If the target sector is within an enemy Air Defence Zone, the SAA is subject to the standard Anti-Air rules if it is engaged.

Air Defence Learn how Anti-Air systems engage aircraft operating within their Air Defence Zones.

Strategic Role

Strategic Airlift provides commanders with a rapid method of repositioning ground forces and deploying formations across long distances.

Standard airlift provides safe strategic redeployment, while airdropping allows forces to be inserted directly into areas where conventional deployment may not be possible.

The vulnerability of SAA operations to enemy Air Defence makes route planning and airspace control important when conducting airdrop operations.
