Special Units

Special Units provide specialised military capabilities that operate outside the standard roles of conventional Army and Air Force formations. These units are designed to perform specific tactical, operational, or defensive functions.

Special Units can provide capabilities such as long-range fire support, air defence, rotary-wing operations, underwater warfare, and coastal defence. Their effectiveness often depends on how they are integrated with conventional forces and the wider military system.

Specialised Forces

Special Units are organised around specific battlefield capabilities rather than serving as general-purpose formations. Each unit type fulfils a distinct role and can provide capabilities that conventional forces cannot easily replicate.

Artillery

Artillery provides long-range fire support for friendly ground forces. Artillery units can engage enemy ground formations without requiring direct contact with the target.

Their ability to attack from a distance makes artillery particularly valuable for supporting frontline formations, disrupting enemy positions, and influencing the battlefield beyond the immediate frontline.

Artillery Learn about artillery range, attacks, suppression, destruction, and counter-battery fire.

Air Defence

Air Defence units protect friendly forces and territory from hostile aircraft. They establish defensive coverage around their position and automatically engage enemy aircraft that enter their effective range.

Air Defence plays an important role in protecting strategic locations, military formations, and other high-value assets from enemy air operations.

Air Defence Explore Anti-Air systems, Air Defence Zones, and their interaction with aircraft.

Rotary Warfare

Rotary-wing forces provide specialised aerial capabilities that complement conventional ground and fixed-wing forces.

Helicopters can perform a variety of battlefield missions, including direct support of ground forces, rapid movement, and specialised combat operations.

Rotary Warfare Learn about helicopter operations and the specialised capabilities of rotary-wing forces.

Submarines

Submarines are specialised naval units capable of operating beneath the ocean surface. Their ability to operate underwater provides a distinct capability compared with conventional surface vessels.

Submarines can operate within maritime areas while remaining beneath the surface, allowing them to perform specialised naval missions and engage enemy forces from an underwater position.

Submarines Explore submarine operations and underwater warfare.

Coastal Defence

Coastal Defence units provide specialised protection for coastal areas and important maritime approaches. They are designed to help defend territory against hostile naval operations.

Coastal Defence provides nations with the ability to protect important coastal sectors without requiring the deployment of a conventional naval fleet to every threatened area.

Coastal Defence Explore coastal defence units and their role in protecting maritime approaches.

Role in Combined Arms

Special Units are most effective when integrated with conventional military forces.

Artillery can provide long-range fire support for frontline formations, Air Defence can protect forces from hostile aircraft, and rotary-wing forces can provide additional mobility and battlefield support.

Submarines and Coastal Defence units provide specialised maritime capabilities that complement conventional naval forces and allow commanders to defend important sea areas and coastal approaches.

Coordinating these specialised capabilities with conventional forces allows commanders to create more effective combined arms formations.

Specialised Capabilities

Special Units generally provide capabilities that cannot be replicated by standard combat formations.

Because of this, their value often comes from how and where they are deployed rather than simply from their individual combat strength.

Commanders should consider the operational environment, supporting forces, and objectives of an operation when deploying Special Units.
