Air-to-Ground Combat

Air-to-Ground Combat governs attacks conducted by aircraft against eligible enemy ground forces. Aircraft must first survive any applicable air defence or interception before resolving their attack against the target.

Eligible Targets

Aircraft may conduct air-to-ground attacks against eligible enemy ground units within their operational range.

Air-to-Ground Capable Units

The following aircraft are capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks:

Multi-Role Fighter Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks. Attack Fighter Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks. Bomber Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks. Strategic Bomber Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks. Stealth Multi-Role Fighter Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks. Naval Multi-Role Fighter Capable of conducting Air-to-Ground attacks.

Air Defence and Interception

Enemy air defence and interception are resolved before the air-to-ground attack takes place.

The aircraft must survive these defensive engagements in order to conduct its attack against the ground target.

Aircraft Damage

If an aircraft is hit by enemy air defence or interception, it immediately suffers one formation size loss.

Aircraft formation damage is caused by effects such as air defence and interception, rather than simply losing an air-to-ground combat roll.

Destroyed Aircraft

If an aircraft is destroyed during air defence or interception, its air-to-ground attack does not occur.

Only aircraft that survive the defensive engagement may continue to resolve their air-to-ground attack.

Air-to-Ground Combat Roll

A surviving aircraft resolves its attack against the target's Defence using the aircraft's Strength.

Both the attacking aircraft and defending ground unit make their respective combat rolls using their current combat values.

01

Aircraft

Rolls using its current Strength.

02

Ground Unit

Rolls using its current Defence.

Attack Outcome

If the aircraft's attack roll is higher than the target's Defence roll, the attack is successful and the target suffers one formation size loss.

If the target's Defence roll is higher than the aircraft's attack roll, the attack has no effect.

If both rolls are equal, the attack has no effect.

Formation Size Loss

A formation size loss moves the affected unit to its next smaller formation level.

Formation size reductions represent losses to the formation and reduce its combat effectiveness according to the applicable formation rules.

Destruction

If a formation at its smallest available formation level suffers another formation size loss, the formation is destroyed.

Destroyed formations are removed from play.

Aircraft Combat Damage

Aircraft do not suffer formation size loss simply because they lose an air-to-ground combat roll.

Aircraft formation damage is caused by effects such as air defence and interception.

Combat Effectiveness

Reducing a formation's size reduces its combat effectiveness according to the applicable formation rules.

Attack Sequence

Air-to-ground attacks follow a defined sequence to determine whether the aircraft successfully strikes its target.

01

Defensive Engagement

Enemy air defence and interception are resolved.

02

Aircraft Survival

The aircraft suffers damage or is destroyed if affected by the defensive engagement.

03

Attack Roll

A surviving aircraft rolls against the target's Defence.

04

Resolution

A successful attack reduces the target's formation size.
