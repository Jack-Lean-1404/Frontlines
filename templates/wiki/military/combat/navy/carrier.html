Carrier Operations

Aircraft Carriers provide mobile air bases that allow nations to project air power far beyond the range of land-based airports. Carriers combine naval mobility with the ability to operate Naval Multi-Role Fighters at sea.

Carriers are specialised naval units that support fleet operations by providing an operational base for embarked aircraft while also providing Anti-Air defence against hostile aircraft.

Carrier Aircraft

Carriers can only operate Naval Multi-Role Fighters as embarked aircraft.

Naval Multi-Role Fighters may launch from and return to their Carrier, allowing the Carrier to function as an operational airport at sea.

Carrier Capacity

A Carrier can carry a number of air units equal to its own formation size. Each embarked air unit can be the same size or smaller than the Carrier.

Carrier Size Air Units Maximum Air Unit Size
Size 1 1 Size 1
Size 2 2 Size 2
Size 3 3 Size 3

For example, a Size 3 Carrier can carry up to 3 air units, and each of those air units may be Size 3 or smaller.

Carrier-Based Air Operations

A Carrier acts as an airport for its embarked Naval Multi-Role Fighters.

Aircraft operating from a Carrier follow the normal aircraft operational rules, including their defined operational range.

The Carrier therefore allows Naval Multi-Role Fighters to conduct air operations far from friendly land-based airports.

Carrier Air Defence

Carriers function as Anti-Air units and provide an Air Defence Zone around themselves.

Enemy aircraft entering the Carrier's Air Defence Zone are automatically engaged according to the normal Anti-Air combat rules.

Air Defence Learn about Anti-Air units, Air Defence Zones, and aircraft interception.

Carrier Combat Restrictions

Carriers cannot directly attack enemy naval units or ground units.

Their offensive capability comes from their embarked Naval Multi-Role Fighters, while their own defensive capability comes from their Anti-Air systems.

Carriers therefore rely on escorts, supporting naval forces, and embarked aircraft when operating in hostile waters.

Carrier Destruction

If a Carrier is destroyed while carrying aircraft, the embarked aircraft are subject to the normal aircraft anchor-point rules.

Any aircraft that cannot reach another friendly Carrier or Airport within its operational range is destroyed.

Aircraft that remain within range of another valid Carrier or Airport are not destroyed and may continue operating from that anchor point.

Strategic Role

Carriers allow nations to project air power across the oceans without relying entirely on land-based airports.

Their combination of mobile aircraft operations and Anti-Air defence makes them valuable assets for fleet protection, maritime operations, and power projection.

Because Carriers cannot directly attack enemy ships or ground forces, they depend on their embarked aircraft and accompanying naval forces to provide offensive capability.
