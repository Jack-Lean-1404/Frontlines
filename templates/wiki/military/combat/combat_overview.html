Combat

Combat represents military engagements between opposing forces in Frontlines. Battles can occur across land, sea, and air, with each combat domain using specialised rules based on the capabilities of the forces involved.

Combat is a core component of military operations. Commanders must consider the strength and capabilities of their forces, battlefield positioning, terrain, logistics, command, and the specialised abilities available to their units.

The general combat rules apply across the game, while individual combat domains contain additional rules governing specific forms of warfare.

General Combat Rules

All combat follows a common set of principles regardless of the forces involved. These rules establish how engagements are initiated and provide the foundation for resolving combat.

Initiating Combat

Combat is initiated when a unit engages an opposing enemy unit according to the rules of its combat domain.

For conventional ground combat, units may not enter a sector occupied by an enemy unit. Instead, the attacking unit must stop in a sector adjacent to the enemy and may then initiate combat.

Units may move adjacent to enemy forces without restriction. There are no Zones of Control that prevent movement around enemy units beyond the restriction on entering an occupied enemy sector.

Movement must be completed before combat. A unit that has moved may attack, but movement cannot be resumed after combat during the same Action Phase.

Movement Learn how units move, position themselves, and interact with enemy forces before combat.

Combat Resolution

Standard combat uses the relevant offensive and defensive capabilities of the participating forces.

The attacking unit uses its final Attack Strength while the defending unit uses its final Defence Value. Applicable modifiers are determined before the engagement is resolved.

The exact resolution process depends on the type of combat being conducted. Land, naval, air, artillery, air defence, and rotary warfare engagements each contain additional rules specific to their domain.

Combat Modifiers

A unit's effective combat capability can be affected by circumstances surrounding an engagement.

Applicable modifiers may come from unit capabilities, formation status, terrain, command, logistics, supporting forces, or other specialised mechanics.

Domain-specific modifiers are explained on the relevant combat pages.

Formation Size

Some military units operate using formation sizes that represent the overall scale and strength of the formation.

When applicable, damage can reduce a unit's formation size, lowering its overall combat capability. The specific effects of formation reduction depend on the type of unit involved.

Certain domains use formation size as an important part of their combat mechanics. These rules are described in the relevant domain-specific pages.

Combined Arms

Frontlines is designed around combined-arms warfare. Different military forces can support one another and provide capabilities that complement the weaknesses of other units.

Ground formations can be supported by artillery and aircraft, naval forces can support operations near coastal areas, and Air Defence can protect friendly formations from hostile aircraft.

Rotary-wing forces can provide additional mobility and battlefield support, while other Special Units provide specialised offensive and defensive capabilities.

Effective commanders must therefore consider how different forces interact rather than relying solely on the strength of individual units.

Combat and the Battlefield

The battlefield environment can influence how forces approach and conduct combat.

Terrain determines where units can move and can restrict certain unit types. Movement and positioning determine where forces can engage the enemy, while logistics and command influence the ability of forces to maintain effective operations.

Terrain Learn how terrain affects movement and unit access across the battlefield. Movement Learn how units move and position themselves before combat.

Logistics and Command

Combat forces must be properly supplied and coordinated during military operations.

Logistics provides the supplies required to sustain forces, while command and control allows commanders to coordinate units across the battlefield.

Units operating without adequate logistical support or effective command can suffer significant operational disadvantages.

Logistics Learn how military forces are supplied and sustained during operations. Command & Control Learn how Headquarters and command networks support military operations.

Specialised Combat

Some combat systems operate outside conventional direct engagements. These systems provide specialised capabilities that can significantly influence the battlefield.

Artillery can engage enemy ground forces from a distance, Anti-Air systems can automatically engage hostile aircraft, and rotary-wing forces can perform specialised battlefield operations.

These mechanics are documented separately so that each combat system can contain its complete rules without unnecessarily expanding the general Combat overview.
