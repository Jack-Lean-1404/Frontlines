Amphibious Warfare

Amphibious Warfare allows nations to project ground forces from the sea onto coastal sectors. These operations allow commanders to establish beachheads, open new fronts, and bypass existing land defences.

Amphibious assaults are inherently difficult operations. Moving forces from ships onto a defended coastline exposes troops to significant risk, making preparation and naval control important to a successful landing.

Amphibious Assault Ship

Amphibious operations require an Amphibious Assault Ship (AAS).

The AAS must be located in a Shallow Water sector that is adjacent to a land sector.

The AAS must also have at least 1 Movement Point remaining to conduct an amphibious assault.

Amphibious Assault Ship Learn about the naval unit used to transport and deploy ground forces during amphibious operations.

Transport Capacity

An Amphibious Assault Ship can carry ground units up to its own size.

The number of ground units an AAS can transport is also determined by its size.

For example, a Size 3 AAS can carry up to 3 ground units, provided each unit is Size 3 or smaller.

An AAS cannot carry more units than its own size.

Landing Requirements

To conduct an amphibious assault, the AAS must be positioned in shallow water directly adjacent to land.

The AAS must have at least 1 Movement Point remaining to perform the assault.

The transported ground forces are then deployed from the AAS onto the adjacent land sector.

Uncontested Landing

If there are no enemy units in any sector adjacent to the AAS, the amphibious operation is automatically successful.

All transported ground units land without casualties.

Contested Landing

If enemy units are present in any sector adjacent to the AAS, the amphibious landing is considered contested.

Each friendly ground unit participating in the amphibious operation has a 50% chance of being destroyed when landing.

Each transported unit resolves its destruction chance independently.

Beachheads

Establishing a beachhead is an important objective of an amphibious operation. Successfully landing forces allows ground formations to establish a presence on the coastline and begin subsequent land operations.

Amphibious operations therefore provide a method of opening new fronts and bypassing conventional land approaches.

Strategic Considerations

Amphibious assaults are high-risk operations, particularly when enemy forces are positioned near the landing area.

Securing surrounding sea sectors, protecting the Amphibious Assault Ship, and reducing nearby enemy forces can improve the strategic conditions for a landing.

Because each transported unit independently faces a 50% destruction chance during a contested landing, committing large formations to a defended coastline carries significant risk.
