Littoral Combat Ship

SEA

Overview

Littoral Combat Ships are small, highly mobile surface combatants designed primarily for operations in coastal and shallow waters. Their combination of mobility and combat capability makes them useful for patrol, escort, and coastal operations.

Role

The primary role of the Littoral Combat Ship is to provide a flexible surface combat capability in coastal environments. LCS units can conduct patrol operations, support larger naval formations, and engage enemy surface forces.

Strengths

  • Low construction cost.
  • High mobility for a surface combatant.
  • Effective for patrol and coastal operations.
  • Well suited to operations near shallow sectors.

Weaknesses

  • Limited defensive capability.
  • Lower combat power than larger surface combatants.
  • Vulnerable when operating against heavier naval formations.
  • Limited ability to operate far from shallow waters.

Special Capability

Littoral Combat Ships are designed primarily for coastal and shallow-water operations.

An LCS can only travel 1 ocean sector away from a shallow sector. It cannot continue farther into deep ocean without remaining within this limit.

Recommended Employment

Use Littoral Combat Ships for coastal patrols, escort duties, and supporting larger naval formations. Their relatively low cost and high mobility make them useful for maintaining a naval presence near coastal and shallow areas while responding quickly to local threats.
