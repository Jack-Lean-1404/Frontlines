Army

The Army is the primary land warfare force in Frontlines. Ground units are responsible for capturing, defending, and controlling sectors while conducting offensive and defensive operations across the battlefield.

Ground Forces

Army forces are organised into combat and support formations. Combat units provide the primary fighting capability of a ground force, while support units provide logistics, engineering, and command capabilities.

Effective land operations require the coordination of multiple unit types. Infantry can secure territory, armoured formations provide offensive power, and specialised support formations allow forces to operate effectively over extended distances.

Combat Units

Combat units form the core of the Army's fighting strength. They directly participate in ground combat and are responsible for taking and holding sectors.

Motorised Infantry Mobile infantry designed to rapidly move across the battlefield. Mechanised Infantry Armoured infantry formations combining mobility, protection, and combat capability. Armoured Heavy armoured formations designed to provide significant offensive and breakthrough capability. National Guard Defensive formations used to protect territory and important positions.

Support Units

Support units provide capabilities that allow combat forces to operate effectively. They generally support combat formations rather than serving as the primary offensive force.

Engineer Provides engineering capabilities and assists friendly forces with river crossings. Logistics Provides supply to friendly combat formations operating away from established supply sources. HQ Provides command and coordination to friendly combat formations.

Unit Size

Army formations operate at different size levels. Unit size represents the scale of a formation and affects several military mechanics, including supply, command, and transport.

Larger formations generally require larger support capabilities. Logistics units must be sufficiently large to supply the formations they support, while Headquarters must be large enough to command the units operating within their command range.

Movement

Army units move between adjacent sectors during the Action Phase. Each unit has a Movement value that determines how far it can normally travel during its turn.

Terrain can affect the movement cost of a unit. Rivers and other geographical features may also impose additional movement requirements or require specialised assistance.

Ground Combat

Army units engage enemy ground forces through land combat. Combat is resolved using the attacking unit's final Attack Strength against the defending unit's final Defence Value.

Combat outcomes can result in formation size reductions, destruction, or other effects depending on the type of engagement.

Army Operations

Army operations depend on more than combat strength. Forces must maintain supply, remain within command range, and coordinate movement across the battlefield.

Engineers, Logistics units, and Headquarters provide important support that allows combat formations to continue operating effectively as they advance.
