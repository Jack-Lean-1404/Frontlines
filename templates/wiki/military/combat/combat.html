Combat

Combat represents military engagements between opposing forces in Frontlines. Battles are resolved using the capabilities of the participating units, applicable modifiers, and the rules governing the type of engagement.

Combat Overview

Combat occurs when a unit initiates an attack against an opposing force. Different military domains use different combat systems, with separate rules governing land, air, and naval engagements.

Before combat is resolved, applicable modifiers are determined. These can include terrain, supply, command status, and specialised unit abilities.

Combat and Movement

Movement must be completed before combat can begin.

A unit may move during a turn and then initiate combat against an eligible enemy unit. Once combat begins, the unit cannot resume movement during that turn.

Units cannot enter sectors occupied by enemy forces. Instead, they must stop in an adjacent sector and initiate combat from there.

Land Combat

Ground combat is resolved using an opposed roll between the attacking and defending forces.

The attacking force uses its final Attack Strength, while the defending force uses its final Defence Value. Each side then makes a random roll between zero and its applicable value.

The higher result determines the outcome of the engagement. The losing side suffers a reduction in formation size.

If both sides roll the same result, both forces lose one formation size.

Land Combat Learn the detailed rules for ground engagements, attacks, defence, and formation losses.

Formation Losses

Combat can reduce the size of the participating formations. A unit that loses a size moves to the next smaller formation level.

If a unit is already at its smallest available formation and suffers another size loss, the unit is destroyed.

Multiple units may participate in attacks against the same sector, allowing forces to concentrate their combat power against an opposing formation.

Armoured Breakthrough

Armoured and Mechanised formations have specialised breakthrough capabilities that allow them to exploit successful attacks and create openings in enemy lines.

Breakthrough mechanics are dependent on the applicable unit types and combat conditions and are described in the detailed land combat rules.

Artillery

Artillery provides indirect fire capability, allowing attacks against enemy ground forces without requiring the artillery unit to occupy the same sector as its target.

Artillery can engage targets at a range of two sectors. Towed artillery must spend its available movement to conduct fire, while Mechanised Artillery can fire without spending movement.

Artillery fire can suppress or damage enemy formations and may also result in destruction depending on the outcome of the attack.

Artillery Learn about indirect fire, suppression, artillery range, and counter-battery fire.

Air Combat

Air combat governs engagements between opposing aerial forces. Aircraft operate from airports or other applicable operational anchors and are affected by aircraft range and contested airspace.

Air operations can include air superiority, interception, strike missions, strategic bombing, and specialised support operations.

Air Combat Learn about aerial engagements, air superiority, aircraft range, and air operations.

Air Defence

Air Defence units protect sectors against hostile aircraft. Anti-Air units establish Air Defence Zones that can engage enemy aircraft operating within their coverage.

Air defence interacts with aircraft capabilities such as stealth and specialised suppression of enemy air defences.

Air Defence Learn how Anti-Air units, Air Defence Zones, stealth, and SEAD interact.

Naval Combat

Naval combat governs engagements between opposing naval forces. Surface combatants, submarines, and specialised naval units each have different roles within maritime warfare.

Naval operations also depend on supply, detection, and specialised capabilities such as Anti-Submarine Warfare.

Naval Combat Learn about surface warfare, submarines, detection, and Anti-Submarine Warfare.

Supply and Combat

Supply is essential to maintaining combat effectiveness. Units suffering from Supply Shortage cannot attack or defend and are restricted to moving only one sector per turn.

Maintaining supply lines is therefore essential when conducting prolonged operations or advancing into enemy territory.

Logistics Learn how military forces receive supplies and how Supply Shortage affects operations.

Command and Combat

Command status also affects combat effectiveness. Units that operate outside the command range of a suitable Headquarters become Disorganised.

Disorganised units deal half damage and have half defence, with fractions rounded down. They are also restricted to moving only one sector per turn.

Command & Control Learn how Headquarters command military forces and how Disorganisation affects combat effectiveness.

Combined Arms

Frontlines encourages the use of combined arms rather than relying on a single unit type. Infantry, armoured formations, artillery, aircraft, naval forces, and support units can perform complementary roles during military operations.

Effective coordination of combat forces, logistics, movement, and command can determine the outcome of an engagement.
