Air Force

The Air Force provides the primary fixed-wing aviation forces of the nations of Alterra. Air Force units are responsible for conducting air operations, supporting ground and naval forces, and projecting military power across the battlefield.

Air operations allow commanders to influence areas beyond the immediate frontline. Different aircraft provide different capabilities, including air superiority, ground attack, strategic bombing, transport, maritime patrol, and naval operations.

Air Force Units

The Air Force consists of a range of aircraft designed to perform different roles across the battlefield. Each aircraft provides specialised capabilities that can be used independently or as part of a wider military operation.

Effective air operations depend on selecting appropriate aircraft for the mission and coordinating them with friendly Army, Naval, and Special Units.

Air Warfare

Air operations are centred around controlling the airspace, attacking enemy forces, and supporting friendly military operations.

Aircraft can operate across multiple sectors and engage targets according to their capabilities, mission, and effective range.

Air combat allows aircraft to engage hostile aircraft, while air-to-ground operations allow suitable aircraft to attack enemy ground forces and positions.

Air Combat Learn how engagements between opposing aircraft are resolved. Air-to-Ground Attacks Learn how aircraft engage enemy ground forces and positions.

Air Missions

Different aircraft are designed to perform different missions. Selecting the appropriate aircraft for an operation allows commanders to make better use of available air power.

Fighter aircraft can contest the airspace and engage hostile aircraft, while attack aircraft can support ground operations. Bombers can conduct attacks against larger or strategic targets, while strategic airlift aircraft provide long-distance military transport.

Tankers and AWACS provide specialised support to other aircraft, while Marine Patrol Aircraft and Naval Multirole Fighters extend Air Force capabilities into maritime operations.

Air Superiority

Control of the airspace can provide a significant advantage during military operations.

Fighter aircraft can be used to intercept and destroy hostile aircraft, reducing the enemy's ability to conduct air operations.

Maintaining air superiority can also protect friendly aircraft conducting ground attack, bombing, transport, and maritime missions.

Air-to-Ground Operations

Aircraft capable of attacking ground targets can provide direct support to friendly ground forces and conduct attacks against enemy positions.

Air-to-ground operations allow commanders to apply air power against enemy forces without requiring a conventional ground engagement.

Air-to-Ground Attacks Learn how aircraft conduct attacks against enemy ground forces.

Strategic Airlift

Strategic Airlift aircraft provide the ability to move military forces and equipment over long distances.

Airlift allows commanders to rapidly reposition forces, reinforce distant areas, and conduct strategic movements that would otherwise require significant time.

Strategic Airlift Learn how strategic airlift operations move military forces and equipment.

Maritime Operations

The Air Force can also contribute directly to maritime operations. Marine Patrol Aircraft provide specialised aerial capabilities for operating over naval areas, while Naval Multirole Fighters provide flexible combat capabilities in support of naval operations.

These aircraft complement conventional naval forces by extending surveillance, combat power, and other specialised capabilities into maritime areas.

Air Support

Air operations can provide important support to other military forces. Attack aircraft can support Army formations, while fighters can protect friendly aircraft and contest enemy air operations.

Tankers extend aircraft endurance, while AWACS aircraft provide airborne surveillance and command support. Strategic airlift aircraft can also rapidly move forces and equipment between distant areas.

Combined Operations

Air Force operations are most effective when coordinated with other branches of the military.

Aircraft can support Army formations through air-to-ground attacks, protect friendly forces through air superiority, and support Naval operations through maritime patrol and naval aviation.

Coordinating air power with ground and naval forces allows commanders to combine the strengths of different military capabilities and conduct more effective operations.

Air Force Operations

Successful Air Force operations require commanders to consider aircraft capabilities, range, targets, and coordination with other military forces.

Aircraft should be selected according to the objectives of the operation. Fighters can be used to contest enemy aircraft, attack aircraft can support ground operations, bombers can strike strategic targets, and transport aircraft can provide strategic mobility.

Support aircraft such as Tankers and AWACS can enhance the effectiveness of other aircraft, while Marine Patrol Aircraft and Naval Multirole Fighters provide specialised capabilities for maritime operations.

The effectiveness of an air operation therefore depends not only on individual aircraft, but also on how air power is organised and coordinated across the battlefield.
