Army

The Army provides the primary ground forces of the nations of Alterra. Army units are responsible for capturing, defending, and controlling territory while conducting offensive and defensive operations across the battlefield.

Army forces combine different unit types to conduct ground operations. Each unit provides different capabilities, allowing commanders to build formations suited to different operational requirements.

Army Units

The Army consists of a range of units designed to conduct ground operations across the battlefield. Units can be used to capture and defend territory, engage enemy forces, manoeuvre across the battlefield, and support wider military operations.

Different units provide different combinations of mobility, protection, firepower, and specialised capabilities. Effective Army operations depend on using these capabilities together.

Land Warfare

Army operations are centred around controlling territory and defeating opposing ground forces.

Ground formations manoeuvre between sectors while accounting for terrain, movement costs, enemy positions, supply, and command support.

When opposing ground forces engage, the engagement is resolved using the land combat system.

Land Combat Learn how engagements between opposing ground forces are resolved.

Movement

Army units move between sectors during the Action Phase. A unit's Movement value determines how far it can normally travel, while terrain and other restrictions can affect where and how it moves.

Movement is an important part of ground operations, allowing commanders to position formations, reinforce threatened sectors, and manoeuvre around enemy forces.

Movement must be completed before combat. Once a unit has initiated combat, it cannot resume movement during the same phase.

Logistics

Army formations require logistical support to maintain sustained operations in the field.

Logistics units and infrastructure provide supply to eligible forces, allowing military formations to operate away from established infrastructure.

Maintaining an effective supply system is essential for prolonged offensive and defensive operations.

Logistics Learn how military forces are supplied and sustained during operations.

Command & Control

Army formations operate more effectively when supported by an appropriate command structure.

Headquarters provide command and coordination to friendly forces within their effective range. Units operating outside suitable command support can become Disorganised and suffer reduced effectiveness.

Command & Control Learn how Headquarters coordinate friendly forces and how Disorganisation affects units.

Combined Arms

Army operations are most effective when different unit types are used together.

Infantry, mechanised formations, armoured forces, and specialised units each provide different capabilities. Coordinating these forces allows commanders to exploit the strengths of individual units while reducing their weaknesses.

Special Units can also complement Army formations by providing capabilities such as long-range fire support, air defence, and other specialised battlefield functions.

Special Units Explore specialised military capabilities that complement conventional Army formations.

Army Operations

Successful Army operations require more than simply concentrating military units. Commanders must consider movement, terrain, supply, command support, and the composition of their formations.

Offensive operations can be used to capture enemy territory and destroy opposing formations, while defensive operations seek to hold important sectors and prevent enemy advances.

The effectiveness of an Army formation therefore depends on both the capabilities of its units and how those units are organised and supported.
